Into The Caves of Qud

Caves of Qud is one of my favourite games of all time. It's very complex and can be a bit difficult to get into, but so much of the complexity comes from basic systems interacting, and once you understand those, the rest comes together surprisingly easily, and you can take your time to enjoy the world.

Click on any of the titles below to expand the section.

Qud has one of the most impressive and thorough gamepad implementations I've ever seen in a game, and doing so in a traditional roguelike is kind of incredible.

You can absolutely play with keyboard or mouse, but gamepad is my preferred way to play, if that says something about the quality of it. So I'm covering it here.

The controls list may seem large, but you really only have to keep a few controls in mind; the four face buttons alone cover 90% of what you'll be doing in-game. And any button with an asterisk* has an onscreen reminder, so you don't have to remember them, so just give this a skim.

I have one suggestion for an alternate binding below, for B.

RS: LOOK

Right Stick: lets you Look by moving a cursor. While looking, you can press A to get a list of whatever's on a tile, or B to stop looking.

LS: POINT

Left Stick: is used to indicate a direction.
Right Trigger: then moves you in that direction.

I know it sounds annoying to have to hit RT every step, but you can hold it and the game will stop you if danger shows up. Trust me, you will be happy to have the precision of movement when you need to step carefully. 

DPAD: NAVIGATE*

DPad is mainly used to navigate menus, but when you're not in a menu:
DPad Left and Right: Move left/right on the ability bar at the bottom of the screen.
DPad Down and Up: Automatically walk to the nearest stairs down/up, and once you get there, go down/up the stairs.

If you're not underground, DPad Up takes you 'upstairs' to the World Map.

 

Other than looking and pointing, there are a few buttons that do a few basic actions, and (LT+button) is almost always an advanced version of that same action.

A: INTERACT

A: Interact with whatever's nearby (The game prioritizes stuff well.)
LT+A: Specify a direction to interact (Then hit A again.)

Neutral stick, like Numpad 5, means "the tile you're currently standing on".

B: WAIT

B: By default, is bound to "Wait A Turn". I recommend that you rebind B to be "Rest Until Healed". It's one of the most valuable actions you can take, and if you're already healed to full, it does the exact same thing as "Wait A Turn".
LT+B: Open the "Wait" menu to specify how long to wait.

Y: AUTOMOVE

Y: Autoexplore (Until you're done or there's danger).
LT+Y: Autowalk (It'll ask you to indicate an edge of zone to autowalk to).
LSB: Open list of nearby points of interest to autowalk to (merchants, NPCs, etc).

X: ABILITY*

X: Use currently-selected ability.
LT+X: Open the Ability List to select any ability to use.

You can reorder your abilities in the Ability List to change the order in the ability bar at the bottom of the screen. You can also assign button chord shortcuts for abilities you use often.

RB: SHOOT*

RB: Aim ranged weapon, or fire ranged weapon if already aiming.
LT+RB: Throw grenade (or whatever you have in your throwing slot).
RSB: Reload ranged weapon (do this often).

START: MENU/TRADE*

Start: Usually opens your character sheet, but if you're currently in conversation, you can trade with anyone in the game by pressing this with the dialogue box open.

The game allows you to customize your experience extensively, despite being a fairly traditional roguelike. It can be as hardcore as you want, as smooth as you want, and it mostly does this through mechanics rather than straight difficulty options. How you start makes the largest difference.

CHOOSING A DIFFICULTY

You can change difficulty modes any time. Whichever you choose, start in Joppa for your first few runs.

  • Choose "Roleplay" If you just want to see the story and vibe in the world, but still want some stakes to dungeon diving. Roleplay mode only saves when you enter/exit towns.
  • Choose "Classic" if you don't mind permadeath and/or like rolling characters. 
If you want that permadeath bite but also want to see the story unfold, choose Classic and "graduate" a character to Roleplay mode if they survive Golgotha. You'll know when that is.

CHARACTER CREATION

  • Choose "True Kin" if you want an easier start but less customization. True Kin have better starting stats and skills, and can equip modular cyborg parts to unlock abilities/traits.
  • Choose "Mutant" if you want more customization and power, and don't mind more chaos. Mutant abilities/traits are permanent and somewhat uncontrolled, but can be levelled up to become absurdly powerful.
If Mutant sounds fun but intimidating, try a premade archetype.

STOP - THE GAME IS TURN-BASED

Stop and look at things, take time to inspect stuff.

You don't need to overthink every action, but at least take a sec to read some of that good good text.

MOVE - RUN FOR YOUR LIFE

Run away if your health bar changes colour, if it looks like you might get surrounded, or if you just don't like the vibe. 

Sprint is a default ability, use it often.

HEAL - KEEP YOURSELF ALIVE

Use "Rest Until Healed" whenever you're not in combat. Healing items are either bad, uncommon, or expensive; resting is free.

You'll still want a couple of each basic healing item, but they all have downsides:
- Witchwood Bark can confuse you
- Bandages only heal bleeding
- Salve Injectors heal over time

SEARCH - EXPLORE EACH ZONE

Use Autoexplore to save you time walking around a map, or take your time and wander around and look at things.

Autoexplore stops you if there's any kind of threat. Later, you can adjust what threat level stops you in the Settings.

CAMP - LIGHT FIRES ON ARRIVAL

Whenever you arrive in a new zone after travelling, use the default Make Camp ability to light a campfire and eat. If it says you can't camp because danger is nearby, well, now you know.

You can always eat a scratch meal for free. Later, you can learn to cook fancier meals to give yourself temporary buffs.

BURDEN - YOU CAN'T CARRY EVERYTHING

If you pick up everything to sell, you'll get overburdened quickly, and be completely unable to move. Prioritize lighter things, or more valuable things. "Take All" doesn't pick up corpses, but it picks up everything else. Water is currency, and water is the heaviest thing you'll never be able to go without.

Unprepared food has weight, prepared food doesn't. With the Meal Preparation skill, you can prepare food at campfires.

WANDER - YOU WILL GET LOST

"Lost" is a status effect you can get while traversing the world, that kicks you into a local area and prevents you from returning to the World Map. To get unlost, you'll need to traverse maps until you get your bearings, or find someone sapient to talk to.

There are cheap skills that help avoid being lost, but don't worry about investing much into them; getting lost isn't the worst thing.

WEAPONS - PICK A LANE

Every melee weapon type and ranged weapon type has a skill tree, and scales based on different stats. Pick one melee and one ranged you like the vibe of that matches your general stat build, and focus your skill points on them. If you have to change weapon types later, it's not hard, and some skills work with multiple weapons.

  • Short Blades: multiple hits, inflicting bleed.
  • Long Blades: switchable stances, with stat bonuses.
  • Axes: wide hits, inflicting wounds.
  • Cudgels: heavy hits, inflicting stun.
  • Shields: deflecting attacks, closing distances fast.
  • Pistols: fast shots, dual-wielding.
  • Rifle/Bow: slow shots, with active skills.
  • Heavy: many shots, with stat penalties.

SKILLS - USE YOUR TOOLKIT

Many mutations and cyborg parts come with active skills you can use and passive skills you can toggle. You can also unlock skills in your skill tree, and each skill category always comes with a free skill. Use your skills whenever you can, both in and out of combat.

If you want a bit of an easier start, get Meal Prep and Harvestry, and experiment with cooking simple meals for buffs. If your stats allow it, get Acrobatics to avoid ranged attacks.

REPUTATION - LIVE AND DRINK

Legendary characters are scattered around Qud. You'll know them if you look at them and their description has faction reputation lines, like 'apes hate them because they wrote a bad poem'. Talk to any legendary and you can 'perform the water ritual' to share water (and gossip and secrets) with them, which will gain/lose you reputation with factions who like/dislike them.

You can check the faction scores in your character menu, which determine who is hostile to you. Everything sentient belongs to one or more factions. Even trees; don't piss off the trees.
And never, ever, ever, kill someone you've shared water with.

BEFORE RED ROCK

If you start in Joppa, you might get a quest to go to Red Rock. You could call this a trap, or a test. Before you go to Red Rock, if you want to survive, you will want:

  • A few healing items (witchwood, bandages)
  • Any ranged weapon (bow, musket, pistol)
  • Any light source (torches, glowsphere)
  • Enough armour to have 6 AV (Armour Value)

To get this stuff and to level up a bit so you get some skill points to spend, wander the marshes around Joppa, the red cliffs to the east, and the salt flats to the west. While you do, do some quests for Joppa's tinkerer Argyve to get some experience and level up.

If you see a "salthopper" or a "slumberling", run like hell. If you don't, you'll quickly find out why.

AFTER RED ROCK

Once you've survived Red Rock and/or have the hang of the basics, your next steps will be to make a desert pilgrimage to the Six Day Stilt, and/or seek the wisdom of the residents of Grit Gate.

What those mean is up to you to find out.

A BIT MORE ADVICE

Here's a few scattered things to keep in mind.

  • Actions take time. This may seem obvious, but think of it this way: every item you pick up off the floor takes a turn, and if you pick up ten things at once you won't know what's happened across those ten turns until you find out at the end.
  • Inspecting painted or engraved objects is a great way to learn stories and rumours. Everyone loves to tell stories about the sultans, and some of them might even be true.
  • Unidentified artifacts could be anything from a steel chair to a rocket launcher. If you don't have the skill to identify or fix something, inspect it while trading with a tinker and they can help you out, for a fee.
  • There's a book out there somewhere with folk remedies for certain illnesses. Keep an eye out for a copy you can carry with you.

LOOKING EAST

You don't have to do anything specific in this game. You can follow the story and discover a broadening plot that's often compared to Morrowind... or you can just wander the world, sharing rumours, chasing artifacts, fighting Templars, trading recipes.

That freedom in itself can be intimidating, but if you want to enjoy Caves of Qud, just look for unique stuff out in the world and make your way there, the same way your character would. I can't say you'll have a good time, but you'll definitely have a time.
